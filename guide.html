How a part is written,
from blank page to upload

This is the whole process in words. The kit is the same process with the actual folder.

01Pick a power nobody has used

A Second Dark power is small, physical, and about you — not "fly", but "your shadow lags behind you by three seconds". The best ones are things you already do (hear, step, copy, freeze) with one screw turned.

The rule that keeps the series fresh: one power per part, never reused. Keep a running slug list — every new idea gets checked against it before you write a word. Ten minutes with a list of "powers done to death" (invisibility, time stop, flying) saves you an episode that feels borrowed.

Test: can you demo it three times in different places — a friend, a stranger, a crowd? If yes, it's a Second Dark power. If you can only demo it once, it's a premise, not a power.

02Write the seven beats

The script is about 90 words. Not 60, not 140. Eight lines, each one a beat:

Beats 1-3 — the cold open, three demos   (no title card yet)
   demo 1: a friend        "Your mom slides you an old photo of her at seven."
   demo 1b: the payoff     "Step in. Her childhood kitchen is real."
   demo 2: somewhere public"The class photo puts you in it, front row."
   demo 3: the cost creeps in "But you only leave when the shutter clicks."

Beat 4 — the title card                  "What if you could step in photos?"

Beats 5-6 — the turn: one real fact      "One catch: the first photo ever took
                                           eight hours to expose."
                                          "Anything that moved became a ghost.
                                           That's real."

Beat 7 — the question                    "So whose photo would you step into,
                                           and what if nobody clicks the shutter?"

Write the demos as commands to "you". Second person, present tense, no names to remember. Each demo is one sentence; each payoff is one sentence. If a line needs a comma to survive, it needs a rewrite.

The science fact is the spine. Every part has exactly one real, checkable fact that makes the power uncomfortable — lies a day (psychology, 2010), eight-hour exposures (Niépce, 1826), pain that exists to protect you. One per part. If the fact could be the plot of the episode, you don't need the power.

03Write the episode spec

Each part lives in one JSON file — script, science, cast, shots, edit, sound. This is the whole spec for the photo episode, abridged:

{
 "id": "StepPhotos",  "slug": "step-photos",
 "topic": "What if you could step in photos?",
 "title": ["WHAT IF YOU COULD", "STEP IN PHOTOS?"],
 "accent": "PHOTOS",   "part": 43,
 "script": [ { "beat": "...", "text": "..." }, ... ],
 "science": [ { "claim": "...", "working": "...", "source": "..." } ],
 "edit": [ { "shot": "S2", "at": "Step" }, ... ]
}

Two fields do the most work. script — one object per line, beat labels in plain words. edit — which shot cuts in at which spoken word; the timeline builder lands every cut two frames before its cue word, which is why the edit feels like it's riding the voice.

04Scenes that match the text

The one rule that makes these feel directed instead of generated: the screen shows what the sentence says. "The cookies are warm" needs a kitchen and a cookie, not a generic rooftop. Concretely:

  • Change shot size at every cut — wide, macro insert, medium, close-up. No two neighbouring shots share a size and angle.
  • Cut on the word — the cut lands two frames before its cue word is spoken.
  • Nothing static over ~3 seconds — a push in, a light change, a head turn. The pacer script flags any shot that holds too long.
  • Characters act, they don't pose — a reach with overshoot, a face that swaps through a blink, a step back before the bad news.

05Voice, sound, loudness

Voice is free. Free-tier TTS narrates the script; word timestamps come back with it. No paid key anywhere in the chain.

Sound is synthesized, not sampled. Every effect — a dry camera click, two glass taps, a dusty wind — is generated from a text description and cached by name. Music is one bed: pizzicato and glockenspiel, tucked under the voice.

Loudness is law. The final master targets −14 LUFS, true peak −1.5 dBTP. The master script prints both and refuses to pass until they're right. This is why the parts sound like a channel, not a hobby.

06Package and post

The film renders 1920×1080, then packages vertical: the frame letterboxed over its own blurred background, a part label burned in at the bottom:

ffmpeg -i final.mp4 -filter_complex \
 "[0:v]split=2[a][b];\
  [a]scale=-2:1920,crop=1080:1920,gblur=sigma=45,eq=brightness=-0.30:saturation=0.92[bg];\
  [b]scale=1080:-2[st];[bg][st]overlay=0:656[v];\
  [v]drawtext=text='Part 17':fontsize=96:fontcolor=white:x=(w-text_w)/2:y=1672[v]" \
 -map "[v]" -map 0:a -c:v libx264 -crf 19 -c:a aac out.mp4

Posting is automatic. A caption queue (part number → file → caption → YouTube title/description) plus a scheduled GitHub Action posts one part a day at 6 PM ET, retries at 7:20, and guards against double-posting with a last-posted timestamp. The PC can be off.
